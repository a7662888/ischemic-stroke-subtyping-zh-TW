# Implementation Provenance & Sources (`SOURCES.md`)

Verified `2026-11-27` (Asia/Taipei). This is an independent, browser-only Traditional Chinese (`zh-TW`) implementation of published peer-reviewed ischemic stroke etiologic classification systems. It is **not** an official Massachusetts General Hospital (MGH) CCS website, TOAST/ASCOD official software, or a clinically certified medical device.

## Implementation Decisions

- Every classification engine (`classifyTOAST`, `classifyCCS`, `classifyASCOD`, `evaluateModernExtensions`) operates strictly on structured allowlisted inputs in browser memory.
- **Lacunar infarct size discrepancy** (`≤15 mm` in TOAST 1993 & ASCOD 2013 vs `<20 mm` in SSS-TOAST/CCS 2005–2007 & ESUS MRI-DWI) is modeled explicitly by separating `≤15 mm` and `>15 to ≤20 mm` infarct options so both systems remain faithful to their published thresholds.
- **Ipsilateral `<50%` plaque with ulceration/thrombosis** is treated as **Evident LAA** in CCS 2007 (`Ay 2007` Table 1) and **A1** in ASCOD 2013 (when endoluminal thrombus is present), while remaining below the `>50%` stenosis threshold of classical TOAST 1993 and triggering the **SyNC** (`Goyal 2020`) modern flag.
- **ASCOD `S1` vs `S2`**: Strictly follows `Amarenco 2013` Table 2 — an isolated single `<15 mm` lacunar infarct *without* older lacunes, Fazekas III leukoaraiosis, microbleeds, *état criblé*, or repeated recent TIAs is graded `S2`, whereas adding any of those chronic/recurrent SVD features upgrades to `S1`.
- **ASCOD `D9` vs `D0`**: Strictly follows `Amarenco 2013` Table 2 — in patients aged `<60` years with no `A1/A2/S1/C1/O1`, missing fat-saturated MRI or catheter angiography yields `D9`; otherwise `D0` when no dissection is suspected and adequate extra-/intracranial & cardiac evaluations are performed.
- **TOAST `mitral_stenosis` & ASCOD `bioprosthetic_valve`**: In `Adams 1993` Table 3, mitral stenosis is high-risk only with atrial fibrillation (`Probable`) and medium-risk without AF (`Possible`). In `Amarenco 2013` Table 2, only mechanical valves are explicitly listed under `C1`, so bioprosthetic valves are graded `C2` in ASCOD while remaining high-risk in CCS (`Ay 2005` Table 2).
